Source: a 53-agent research workflow (2026-10-05) with independent checks of key claims against primary sources (trust CORRECTED/REFUTED annotations over the original text). Filtered to the theory of cloaking limits, demonstrated passive devices, frontier loopholes, materials and simulation tools; 68 lines about sensors and applications were removed.

## Fundamental limits on cloaking and invisibility
- Fundamental limits in photonics and electromagnetics: a tutorial, §5.7 (Miller and Monticone, arXiv:2605.24738, May 2026) (2026) [review]: The most current expert synthesis. It proves perfect broadband invisibility impossible for any finite, causal, stable scatterer, active systems included (provided there are no poles on or above the real axis). Proof: zero total SCS on an interval forces zero forward amplitude, which is analytic in the upper half-plane; by the identity theorem it vanishes everywhere, so via the optical theorem σ_ext = 0 at all frequencies, which is impossible for matter. Stated loopholes: (1) prior knowledge of the incident field and its arrival time; (2) time-varying or nonlinear systems, where analyticity in several frequency variables can fail. It reproduces the Craeye–Bhattacharya rule of thumb B < λ0/(ΔR·Qc), with Qc ≈ 5–10 for good cloaking. It shows the spherical-wave sum rule ∫ ln|sn(λ)| dλ ≤ 2π²a is NOT a cloaking bound, because it bounds absorption. Open questions OQ15 and OQ16: rigorous size–bandwidth–performance limits for ANY invisibility effect, given the amount of matter in a volume, remain open. | Numbers: Human-scale example: extra path ΔR ≈ 1 m, Qc = 5 and λ0 = 500 nm give B ≈ 10^-5 % (1e-7). The paper's Eq. 218 example: a = 1 m, 500 nm, 10× suppression gives B ≈ 2e-5 %. | Blocker/opening: Blocker: active LTI cloaks also cannot be perfect over a band. Opening: the only theoretical loopholes are prior knowledge of the field, time variation or nonlinearity, plus the open problem of rigorous general bounds. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24738
- Diameter–bandwidth product limit for isolated-object cloaking (Hashemi, Qiu, McCauley, Joannopoulos and Johnson, PRA 86, 013804, 2012); scaling limits (PRA 84, 023815, 2011) (2012) [theorem_or_bound]: Covers transformation-based isolated-object cloaks with passive, causal materials of bounded index contrast, perfect fabrication and negligible absorption. Using the optical theorem plus contour integration, ⟨σ_tot⟩ averaged over Δω equals Im f(ω_op + iΔω). Causality forces Im ε, Im μ > 0 at complex frequency, so the bandwidth-averaged cross-section scales as ⟨σ_tot⟩/s_g ∝ d, meaning bandwidth falls as 1/diameter. In spherical-cloak numerics, R1 = 2λ and R1 = 4λ have bandwidths 1/2.1 and 1/4.1 of the R1 = λ case. The 2011 paper adds that cloak thickness must scale with object thickness, and absorption or imperfection tolerances must scale as 1/thickness. The proof does not cover ground-plane cloaks, gain, angle-limited incidence or phase-insensitive detection. | Numbers: Bandwidth ∝ 1/d. Doubling the radius from λ to 2λ costs a factor of 2.1 in bandwidth; going to 4λ costs a factor of 4.1. | Blocker/opening: Blocker for isolated phase-preserving cloaks at k0a ~ 10^6–10^7. The authors list the exceptions (ground plane, gain, restricted angles or phase) as the escape routes. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.2190
- Rigorous quasistatic bounds on broadband passive cloaking (Cassier and Milton, J. Math. Phys. 58, 071504, 2017) (2017) [theorem_or_bound]: Uses Herglotz-function sum rules on the polarizability tensor of a cloaking device with an object of any shape, in quasi-statics, with linear passive materials. Result: no passive cloak can cloak an object over a whole frequency band in quasi-statics, with quantitative inequalities limiting the cloaking effect over a finite range. It applies to anomalous-resonance, transformation and complementary-media cloaks. It notes that TO cloaks need relative ε < 1, which forces dispersion, and that reported broadband carpet cloaks work because the background is silicon. Authors' quote: 'There is no theoretical difficulty in creating broadband active cloaks.' Example: active exterior cloaking with 3 or more devices simulated for broadband pulses (Guevara Vasquez, Milton and Onofrei, Opt. Express 17, 14800, 2009). This assumes the incoming field is known or sensed. | Numbers: Applies to quasi-static (electrically small) regime only; no human-scale numbers. | Blocker/opening: Rigorous confirmation of the passive impossibility, but only in quasi-statics. Opening: broadband active cloaking given field knowledge. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08592
- Nonreciprocity alone does not beat the time–bandwidth limit (Mann, Sounas and Alù, Optica 6, 104, 2019) (2019) [theorem_or_bound]: Considers resonant cavities in linear time-invariant systems. Against claims that nonreciprocal cavities overcome the time–bandwidth limit, the authors derive general relations from microscopic reversibility. Authors' quote: 'nonreciprocal cavities do not provide specific advantages in terms of the time-bandwidth limit'. Implication for cloaking: magneto-optic or other static nonreciprocal (LTI) media do not by themselves escape delay–bandwidth or Bode–Fano-type constraints; time modulation or activity is needed. | Numbers: (none; analytic result) | Blocker/opening: Blocker for 'nonreciprocal passive cloak' ideas built on bandwidth arguments. Nonreciprocity remains useful for directional asymmetry (e.g., one-way invisibility, Kirchhoff breaking). | https://doi.org/10.1364/OPTICA.6.000104

Blockers:
  - Visible band, passive, all angles, phase preserved: a human needs fractional bandwidth B = 0.545 (400–700 nm). The Monticone–Alù bound allows B_max ≈ 1.9e-7 to 5.7e-7 for a 10 dB total-scattering reduction (a = 0.54–0.93 m). The shortfall is about 10^6. The Craeye–Bhattacharya rule gives B ≈ 1e-7 for ΔR = 1 m and Qc = 5.
  - LWIR 8–14 μm, passive, all angles, phase preserved: B_max ≈ 3.7e-6 to 1.1e-5 for 10 dB, against B = 0.545 needed, a shortfall of about 10^5. In this band the human signature is mainly emission: 186 W/m² band exitance at 305 K vs 147.5 W/m² for a 290 K background, about 2.75 W m⁻² K⁻¹. Scattering-cancellation cloaks do not address emission at all.
  - Dispersion: a lossless passive medium cannot hold ε < ε0 constant. Over B = 0.545, the minimum deviation is 0.43·(ε0 − εm), which rules out the near-zero or below-1 ε that transformation cloaks need over the visible.
  - Global sum rule: any linear, passive, non-diamagnetic cloak raises the all-spectrum integrated extinction. In a 100 nm-sphere example, plasmonic cloaks raised it 3.2× and transformation-optics cloaks 17.5–20×. A narrowband cloak therefore becomes a beacon to broadband or pulsed probes.
  - Active LTI cloaks still cannot be perfect over any band (identity theorem). Gain-based fast-light cloaks become unstable, i.e. they lase. Non-Foster circuits are only conditionally stable.
  - True causal active cloaks in vacuum have no latency budget, so they are detectable by pulses shorter than the added sensing, compute and propagation delay. Any measurement-based or loss-compensating cloak is detectable by quantum (no-cloning) probes.
  - Time-varying absorbers and matching networks generate out-of-band harmonics that broadband receivers can see. They need external power and synchronization. At optical frequencies they need ENZ materials, which are lossy.
  - Static nonreciprocity (LTI magneto-optics) gives no time–bandwidth advantage (Mann, Sounas and Alù 2019), so it is not a bandwidth escape.
  - Seeing while cloaked: σ_ext ≥ σ_abs ≈ 1.4e-5 m² for two 3 mm pupils. This is nonzero but about 1.6e-5 of the silhouette, a minor constraint.
Openings:
  - Active light-field (camera plus lenticular display) cloaks against the human eye. Being active, they sit outside the passive bounds. Miller's latency limit binds only against time-resolved, coherent or quantum probes, not against human vision. The hard constraints move to light-field sampling density, dynamic range, spectral match and power, all engineering frontiers. They remain detectable by pulsed or interferometric probes.
  - Rigorous bounds as a research product. Open questions OQ15 and OQ16 (Miller and Monticone 2026) ask for rigorous size–bandwidth–performance limits for ANY invisibility effect given fixed matter in a volume, including phase-insensitive and angle-limited metrics. Computable convex-relaxation bounds checked against Meep would tell the engine where devices can go. This is uncontested theory territory.
  - Thermal IR via emission engineering rather than scattering. Nonreciprocal (magneto-optic InAs) emitters already break Kirchhoff's law over 12.5–16 μm at 1 T (tuning about 0.1). Routing emission and metabolic heat away from observer directions, plus matching background radiance (for example an effective emissivity of about 0.67 when reflecting a 250 K sky against a 290 K background), is not forbidden by any cloaking bound. The needs are stronger nonreciprocity at lower field and over wider angles.
  - Static diamagnetism and superconducting thin shells are the only passive exception to the global extinction bound. This is niche, but worth one engine probe for RF and low-frequency signatures.
  - A background with wave speed below c lifts the isolated-object causality bound, as both Hashemi and Monticone note. Underwater optical cloaking (n = 1.33) of large objects is therefore not causality-forbidden, though loss and fabrication limits remain. It is not applicable to a human in air, but it is a useful testbed.

Blockers:
  - Scale gap. A 1.8 m x 0.5 m human is about 3.3e6 x 9e5 wavelengths at 550 nm. The best broadband, polarization-independent 3D phase-preserving optical cloaks hide bumps about 1 um high (Ergin 2010: 1.4-2.7 um, unpolarized, up to 60 degrees; Fischer 2011: down to about 600 nm). The largest macroscopic visible transformation-optics cloaks hide 1.2-38 mm objects (Chen 2011, Zhang 2011, Zheng 2018), each with polarization, direction or background restrictions.
  - Delay-loss. For a 2 m object at 575 nm with a 10 cm cloak and 10% bandwidth, the required loss tangent is << 2e-8; phase-delay bandwidth is 0.013 pm (Hashemi 2010). Measured optical metamaterial cloaks lose far more. Valentine 2009 measured 58% transmission at 1540 nm across a micron-scale silicon cloak because of fabrication disorder. Nanostructured or metallic graded media cannot meet the bound at metre scale.
  - Passive sum rule and bandwidth bounds. Integrated extinction over all wavelengths always increases for linear, passive, causal, non-diamagnetic cloaks (Monticone & Alu 2013). The Bode-Fano bounds (Optica 2016) tighten with object size. Strekha 2024: no finite isotropic cloak is perfect over any nonzero bandwidth, even for a lambda/4-radius object, and performance saturates at a cloak radius of about 2x the object radius for bandwidths of 1e-5 or more. Muehlig 2013 shows the trade-off experimentally: suppression at 360 nm, extra scattering above 400 nm.

Openings:
  - Widen the angular range of ray-optics cloaks by inverse design. The Rochester formalism is exact only to first order. The authors note paraxial validity to about 10-15 degrees (up to 30 degrees), but the hardware reached only several degrees. Freeform surfaces, GRIN elements or multi-element optimized stacks (differentiable ray tracing) aimed at +/-15-30 degrees with suppressed edge effects form a concrete, testable gap with no known fundamental no-go.
  - Polyhedral, piecewise-homogeneous cloaks made of bulk, low-loss, isotropic glasses (Chen 2013; Zheng 2018) avoid nanostructure loss. Intrinsic absorption of ultrapure glass is about 1e-10 loss tangent, below even Hashemi's visible threshold (derived context). Open search problems: more viewing directions than 3 axes or 6 directions, lower mass (thin-shell or Fresnel-segmented prisms), achromatization with complementary-dispersion glass pairs (suggested in Zhang 2011), and anti-reflection coatings.
  - Environment-specific full cloaking. In turbid media (fog, smoke, murky water), diffusive cloaks are already macroscopic, omnidirectional, broadband and polarization-independent (Schittny 2014, 2015). In water (n=1.33), the causality limit on isolated-object cloaks is lifted (Hashemi), and six-direction glass cloaks worked (Chen 2013). These are the physically least constrained routes to hiding a person.
  - Bounds-first evaluation pipeline. Reject impossible wave-regime ideas with the Strekha dual-bound framework and the Monticone-Alu sum rule before simulation. Then validate sub-elements with open-source GPU FDTD with autodiff (FDTDX), Meep or Ceviche, and spherical shells with exact Mie (scattnlay). Score macroscopic assemblies with ray tracing. This gives the idea engine an objective, cheap test harness.
  - Out-of-scope pointer. Every bound above assumes linear, passive, time-invariant media, and both Monticone & Alu and the 2016 Optica analysis point to active, nonlinear or time-varying approaches for broadband cloaking. Those, and camera-display 'digital' cloaks such as Choi & Howell's 2016 digital integral cloak, belong to a separate research dimension.

Blockers:
  - Passive, time-invariant, phase-preserving cloaking of a person in visible light is ruled out by the bounds: 10× scattering reduction for a 1 m sphere at 500 nm is limited to B = 2×10⁻⁷ (Monticone & Alù 2016), about 2.7×10⁶ short of the 400–700 nm band. Bandwidth also falls as 1/diameter (Hashemi 2012), and a person is about 3.6×10⁶ λ across against 10λ for the best full-parameter EM cloak (microwave) and 3λ for the 3D acoustic cloak. The 2026 Cassier–Milton–Welters bound extends this to near-field observers.
  - Time-varying loophole: the only experiment beating a time-invariant bound (RF, 2025) needed modulation depth 0.99 at rates comparable to the 72 MHz signal band. For a visible band about 320 THz wide, that means order-1 index modulation at tens of THz. The best optical demonstration needs about 124 GW/cm² peak for a 40 nm ITO film, as ps-scale transients at 1.3 µm, which is about 1.2 PW peak over 1 m². There is also no experimental time-modulated cloak (as opposed to an absorber) that beats a passive cloaking bound at any frequency.
  - Gain-based routes (PT-symmetric, balanced loss–gain, fast-light, loss-compensated negative-index) exist only as theory, or experimentally on µm-scale chips or films (1550 nm PT chip; 737 nm negative-index film with figure of merit 26). Macroscopic optical gain brings stability limits from causality (Nistad & Skaar 2008), pump power and heat, and spontaneous emission that would make the cloak glow. The fast-light visible cloak is simulation only.
  - Illusion optics needs low-loss negative-index complementary media at a single selected frequency, with loss untreated in theory. The best experiment is a 2D lumped circuit working at 35–60 MHz.
  - Broadband success in other domains does not transfer. Thermal, dc electric and dc magnetic cloaks are broadband only because Laplace and diffusion physics has no wave speed and no phase (the heat equation is invariant under r→sr, t→s²t). Acoustic and seismic broadband results rely on ground (reflection-only) geometry or very long wavelengths (50 Hz).
  - Nonlocal and flat-optics compression trades bandwidth and angle away: the record spaceplate compression R = 176 works only within 1° and 0.055 nm at 1550 nm (widest design: 2.8 nm and 10°). Nonlocality is a tool, not a way past the bounds.
  - Temporal cloaks (46% of the time axis at 12.7 Gb/s) work only for a controlled single-mode probe in fibre. They have no spatial counterpart for incoherent multimode daylight. Doppler cloaks fix shifts of about 10⁻¹ c that a human observer could never see (a walking person gives about 10⁻⁸).
Openings:
  - Most promising for a person in visible light: drop phase and actively re-emit at ray level. The bounds cover coherent full-wave scattering, but eyes sample only intensity per direction in about 3 colour channels at about 1 arcmin (Chen 2013 dropped phase preservation and hid a cat). A sense-and-re-emit light-field cloak (camera plus light-field display) is the ray-level version of the active and balanced loss–gain cloaks (Selvanayagam 2013; Sounas 2015), and the passive bounds do not apply to it. My rough estimate of display rays needed: about 1.2×10⁶ spatial pixels for a 1.8 m × 0.5 m silhouette at 3 m and 1 arcmin; about 10¹⁰ rays for ±60°/±30° views at 1° steps; but only 2 views per tracked observer (about 2×10⁷ rays for 10 observers, comparable to an 8K panel). Test every candidate in the installed R4 rung (Mitsuba 3 plus NVIDIA FLIP) for perceptual detectability before any hardware.
  - Falsification tasks that cut the search space: (a) implement the Monticone–Alù and Hashemi bounds as R0 calculators, so passive, time-invariant, phase-preserving ideas for human-sized visible cloaks are rejected automatically; (b) re-run the fast-light cloak in scattnlay or Meep with a gain model that is consistent with Kramers–Kronig, and check stability (poles in the upper half-plane) and amplified noise. Either result is decisive: if it survives, it is the only full-wave visible broadband route; if it fails, the engine drops it.
  - Neighbouring domain where engineering, not bounds, is the limit: thermal-IR signature control for people (diffusion cloaks have no Bode–Fano analogue, and adaptive IR-reflecting skins exist: Xu et al., Science 359, 1495, 2018). Worth a parallel lane if 'human invisibility' includes thermal cameras.

(caveat: (1) Scope of the model. Eq. 2 comes from an idealized 1D ground-plane cloak hit by a wave at normal incidence. It applies only in the time-delay regime, meaning the delay–bandwidth product is not small. In the narrowband phase-delay regime the loss tolerance does not shrink as h grows, but the bandwidth collapses: the paper gives 0.013 pm for the 2 m visible case. The authors argue that 2D and 3D cases and oblique incidence are stricter, not looser, because oblique paths need more delay. (2) Order of magnitude only. γ ~ ω Im n/Re n is a proportionality ("∼"), and the result is a "≪" bound. Read 4×10⁻⁸ as a scale, not a pass/fail threshold. The paper defines the loss tangent as Im n/Re n. The usual material-datasheet figure is tanδ = ε''/ε', which is about 2·Im n/Re n. Blockers:
  - Passive bandwidth bound: for a 1 m object at 500 nm with 10× scattering reduction, B ≈ 0.00002% (Monticone & Alù 2016). No passive, linear design of any complexity can make a human broadband-invisible in the visible.
  - Delay–loss bound (Hashemi et al. 2010, Eq. 2): the loss tangent must be ≪ 4×10⁻⁸ for a 1 m object at 500 nm. Plasmonic and metamaterial losses are many orders of magnitude higher.
  - Full-wave verification of a human-scale visible cloak is out of reach. A 1.8×0.5×0.3 m box at λ/20 (27.5 nm) is about 1.3×10²² cells; even a 1 mm shell over 1.8 m² is about 8.7×10¹⁹ cells. The largest open run is 2.3×10⁹ cells (fdtdx, 4×H100, 7 h), so the gap is about 10¹⁰–10¹³. Whole-body claims can only be checked hierarchically: local full-wave, then ray optics and rendering. The approximations at each hand-off can introduce errors that must be validated separately.
  - Local machine limits: 8 GB RAM, no NVIDIA GPU, and only 3.0 GB of free disk measured today (99% full; ENV.md assumes about 6 GB). That blocks installing LLVM for Mitsuba's LLVM mode (needs LLVM 18+, about 1.5 GB), installing fdtdx (its dependencies include tidy3d, moviepy and trimesh), and any GPU path. fdtdz and the GPU modes of fdtdx and torcwa need CUDA, so 3D and broadband runs belong on rented H100s.
  - Tool maintenance risk: the fmmax and invrs-gym GitHub repos are archived (last pushes 2025-10-21 and 2025-10-22). ceviche's last release was Feb 2023, and fdtdz's PyPI release is from Oct 2023 with a narrow feature set (lossless dielectrics only, limited z-extent, single GPU). Pin versions, and treat these as frozen tools.
  - Accuracy floors: about 5% disagreement between independent codes for freeform designs (Chen et al. 2024). The local Meep check reaches 7.5% max error at sharp resonances at 40 px/µm. fdtdx's validation against Meep is qualitative only, and it computes approximate fluxes during optimization by default. Resonant or high-Q cloak claims therefore need convergence checks and a second code.
  - Perceptual limits: HDR-VDP-3's detection task is calibrated on simple psychophysical stimuli, with 'potentially lower accuracy for complex images', and it needs images in absolute luminance. Rendered invisibility claims are predictions until tested on human subjects. Mitsuba is geometric optics, so wave effects of subwavelength cloak layers have to be imported as BSDFs from R1–R3.
Openings:
  - Verifier ladder for the engine, ordered by cost so that compute and energy go only to ideas that survive. R0: analytic gates in µs–ms (passivity and causality, the Monticone–Alù bandwidth bound, Hashemi delay–loss, energy conservation, size relative to λ). R0.5: QCQP or conservation-law bounds for a specified design region (Jelinek et al. 2021; Kuang & Miller 2020), minutes. R1: exact semi-analytic solvers in ms–s (scattnlay and treams for core–shell and scattering cancellation, Airy and transfer matrices, FMMAX or meent RCWA for periodic patches, ABCD matrices for ray cloaks). R2: 2D full-wave at one frequency in s–min (ceviche FDFD with autodiff), cross-checked with NGSolve or FEniCSx FEM for anisotropic transformation-optics media; acceptance is two-code agreement within 5% and less than 2% change on resolution doubling. R3: 3D, broadband and angle/polarization sweeps with real dispersive, lossy materials (Meep locally for small cells; fdtdx on an H100, about 26 min for 288M cells × 21k steps; Tidy3D as a spot check). R4: observer renders in Mitsuba 3 with device behaviour imported from R1–R3, scored with HDR-VDP-3 detection probability, ColorVideoVDP for motion, and FLIP for quick checks. R5: physical prototype, microwave first.
  - Hierarchical bridge between R3 and R4. Compute angle-, wavelength- and polarization-resolved S-matrices of cloak or metasurface patches with RCWA (FMMAX's archived but working Jones-direct formulation; my local check matched Airy to 4.4×10⁻¹⁶). Feed them to Mitsuba as BSDFs. That makes an objective, whole-body, human-observer test computationally possible where full-wave is not. The local-periodic approximation should be spot-checked with fdtdx on finite patches.
  - Active, nonlinear and time-varying cloaks are where Monticone & Alù say progress has to come from. Meep already supports nonlinear media and gain. fdtdx is JAX source code, so agents can add time-varying ε(t) or gain terms and differentiate through them. Any such design must also pass power-budget and stability checks (power, noise, causality) at R0.
  - 3D gradient-based inverse design of wavelength-scale broadband cloaks is now affordable with open tools. fdtdx 0.6.2 has Drude–Lorentz dispersion, anisotropy and time-reversal gradients at about 20 GB for 3M cells. One H100 runs a 36M-cell forward simulation in about 1 min 50 s, so a few hundred gradient steps take hours rather than weeks.
  - Benchmark-first agent calibration. Before trusting agent-written simulation scripts on cloak problems, require them to reproduce the Chen et al. 2024 test problems (within 5% of cross-validated values), the invrs-gym baselines, and this repo's exact-answer checks. Then mutation-test the critics on deliberately broken setups (wrong PML, under-resolved grids, sign-convention errors such as exp(−iωt) vs exp(+iωt)). Not yet run: the verification should repeat with 'fresh' invrs-gym challenges the agents have not seen, to avoid overfitting to the benchmark.

Blockers:
  - Loss budget versus best materials. A human-scale broadband visible cloak needs effective Im n/Re n ≲ λ/(4π(h+d)), about 2×10^-8 for a 2 m object (Hashemi 2010). Silver at 550 nm has ε″/|ε′| ≈ 0.016 (epitaxial, Wu 2014) to 0.033 (Johnson & Christy), a gap of about 10^6. Gold is unusable below about 550 nm (Q < 4) and Al has Q ≈ 3–5. The best visible negative-index metamaterial has FOM 3.34 (Im n/Re n ≈ 0.3), a gap of about 10^7. Khurgin's mechanism still applies: about 10 fs electron scattering, Landau damping at about 10 nm confinement, and no help from cooling.
  - Gain does not close the gap. Transparency in tightly confined plasmonics needs more than 1 MA/cm² (Khurgin 2015), or more than 100 kA/cm² for sub-λ/4n waveguides (Khurgin & Sun 2012), at least 1000× laser thresholds. Gain cannot cancel disorder scattering at all, and absorption compensation must become more exact as size grows (Hashemi 2011).
  - No optical μ ≠ 1 as a bulk, low-loss medium. Merlin's condition needs √ε_S ≈ λ/l. The transparent visible ceiling is ε ≈ 11–12 (GaP, n 3.30–3.45 at 550–650 nm; GaP absorbs at 450 nm with k = 0.078). So magnetic meta-atoms are at least λ/3.5 in size and resonant. Deeply subwavelength (λ/10) dielectric magnetic atoms need n ≥ 10 (Soukoulis & Wegener). Metal split-ring magnetism saturates above about 100 THz (Zhou 2005). The natural palette in the visible is ε ∈ [~1.2 (nanoporous SiO2), ~12] with μ = 1. Values of ε < 1 come only from lossy metals or from narrowband resonances, and in transparent regions they also need dispersion (the Landau–Lifshitz inequality, not re-verified here).
  - Transformation-cloak parameter extremes translate directly into residual visibility. The ideal 2D cloak needs eigenvalues running to 0 and ∞ (∝ r and r^-1) at the inner boundary (KSVW 2008). A regularized 3D near-cloak gives residual O(ρ), needs inner radial parameters of about ρ²/2 (my derivation: about 5×10^-5 for 1%), and a lining with loss β ≈ 4ρ^-2. In 2D the residual is O(1/|log ρ|): 10% residual needs ρ ≈ 4.5×10^-5, an anisotropy of about 2×10^9 (my derivation). Ruan 2007 finds |c0| = 0.099 even at δ = 10^-8·a.
  - Fabrication error budget. Systematic index errors, neglected anisotropy and random disorder (α²·d_α) must all scale as 1/object thickness (Hashemi 2011). The best large-area visible metasurface fabrication achieves 0.05–0.08λ RMS wavefront error (Park 2024, 100 mm DUV). Under the Maréchal approximation (my derivation), that leaves about 9–22% of light outside the coherent image. The best single-pass visible TiO2 metasurfaces are 78–82% efficient (Devlin 2016).
  - Volumetric 3D fabrication at human scale is out of reach. Non-slow-light cloak thickness must be at least h/(n−1), about 0.3 m or more for a body-scale object with n_eff ≤ 2 (my derivation from Hashemi 2010). The fastest 3D printing is 7×10^6 voxels/s at 0.55 µm³, about 14 mm³/h (Hahn 2022). DLW resolution is about 100 nm (about 50 nm with STED), and projection two-photon printing reaches ≥ 175 nm. A 0.6 m³ cloak would take about 4×10^7 h at too-coarse resolution (my estimate). Visible 3D carpet cloaks already fail near the woodpile stop band around 575 nm (Fischer 2011).
  - Bandwidth in loss-tolerant regimes. In the phase-delay regime the loss tolerance stops scaling with size, but the bandwidth for a human-scale visible cloak drops to about 0.013 pm (Hashemi 2010). The demonstrated visible skin cloak works at a single wavelength (730 nm, Ni 2015).
  - Measurement floor. Ellipsometric data set k ≈ 0 only to about 10^-3 to 10^-4, so tabulated 'lossless' visible dielectrics are not verified anywhere near the 10^-8 needed. Thin-film absorption, sidewall roughness and grain scattering in TiO2, GaP or SiN nanostructures need direct low-loss metrology. Hashemi notes that wavelength-scale photonic structures usually reach only cm-scale decay lengths, versus about 1 km at a 10^-9 loss tangent.
Openings:
  - Disorder-aware inverse design measured as effective loss. Hashemi's result reduces visibility to an effective Im n/Re n that includes disorder (α²·d_α ∝ 1/size). That is an objective, simulatable metric. Monte-Carlo sidewall roughness, CD (feature-width) error and etch-depth variation, e.g. Park 2024's 5% etch-depth spread and < 45 nm overlay, can be put into open FDTD/RCWA solvers (Meep, S4) or GPU FDTD to rank designs by effective loss tangent, scattering and residual phase error before any fabrication.
  - Large-area 2D metasurface skins are now manufacturable. Roll-to-roll nanoimprint makes 300 visible metalenses per second at a cost the authors put at or below refractive optics (Hoang, Nature 2026). 12″ ArF masters yield hundreds of cm-scale lenses (Kim 2023). Continuous 100 mm visible metasurfaces reach 0.05λ RMS with industry masks (Park 2024). Thermoforming gives wafer-scale curved metasurfaces with mm-level radii of curvature and µm alignment (Martin-Monier et al., Nat. Commun. Sept 2026, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77862-w; operating band not checked). A conformal, phase-restoring or limited-view skin over about 1.9 m² is therefore a materials-and-physics problem (efficiency, bandwidth, angle), not an area or cost problem.
  - Materials discovery for low-loss negative or near-zero permittivity in the visible. Khurgin calls a concerted search for man-made low-loss negative-ε materials the 'most exciting' direction. Physics caveats: Landau damping (γ ~ v_F/a) persists in any metal, so the candidates need band-gap-protected or low-carrier-density mechanisms. Even a 10× loss reduction leaves about 10^5 to the human-scale budget, so this matters for small or limited-angle devices, not body cloaks.
