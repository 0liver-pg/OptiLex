## Miller & Monticone 2026 tutorial: cloaking limits and open questions

- **Theorem: perfect invisibility is impossible over any nonzero bandwidth (Sec. 5.7, pp. 119-120, Fig. 40)**: Suppose total sigma_scat(omega) = 0 on a real interval. Then the far-field amplitude vanishes in every direction there. Footnote 63: for a finite scatterer the far field is a smooth function of angle, so 'almost everywhere' becomes 'everywhere'. In particular the forward amplitude s(omega,0,phi) vanishes. That amplitude is analytic for Im omega > 0, and with no poles or singularities on the real axis it continues analytically to a neighbourhood of the interval. By the identity theorem it is then identically zero. The optical theorem (Eq. 164) gives sigma_ext = 0 at all real frequencies, hence sigma_scat = 0 everywhere, which no material object satisfies. Footnote 65 closes the gain loophole: compensating everywhere would need net gain at every frequency, which the positivity of the f-sum rule (Eq. 59) forbids. | *Assumptions:* The object is finite and isolated in free space. Linear, time-invariant, causal and stable. Active media are allowed provided there are no scattering poles or singularities on or above the real axis (fn. 64). The argument can fail for objects infinite in one or more directions, whose angular pattern can become discontinuous. Loopholes named in fn. 65: (1) an active scatterer with prior knowledge of the incident field and its arrival time can cancel the scattered field over any bandwidth [357, Selvanayagam & Eleftheriades PRX 2013]; (2) in time-varying or nonlinear systems the amplitude depends on several frequencies, which can break the analyticity argument. | *Numbers:* None. Scattering can vanish only at a discrete set of frequencies (Fig. 40). | *Applicability:* Rigorous, but qualitative only: it says perfect invisibility over a band is impossible and gives no residual-versus-bandwidth number. Good as a sanity gate: any 'bound' that permits zero sigma_scat on an interval for an LTI passive object is wrong. It covers every invisibility effect, not only cloaks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24738
- **Single-frequency invisibility and the absorption-scattering trade-off (Eqs. 199-203, Fig. 39)**: At one frequency, perfect invisibility needs zero absorption: if sigma_abs > 0 then sigma_ext > 0, and the optical theorem forces a nonzero forward amplitude, i.e. a shadow. Spherically symmetric objects: passivity gives |s_n| <= 1, with Mie coefficient c_n = (s_n - 1)/2 (fn. 60). Per harmonic, sigma_scat,n = (pi/(2k0^2))(2n+1)[|s_n^TM - 1|^2 + |s_n^TE - 1|^2], sigma_abs,n = (pi/(2k0^2))(2n+1)[1 - |s_n^TE|^2 + 1 - |s_n^TM|^2], and the ratio (sigma_abs/sigma_scat)_n = (1 - |s_n|^2)/|s_n - 1|^2 (Eq. 202). At any absorption level this sets the minimum and maximum scattering per channel. Eq. 203 [antenna directivity trade-off] is printed as sigma_abs + sigma_scat <= sqrt(sigma_scat D_s(k_i)/pi). | *Assumptions:* Single frequency, passive. Eqs. 199-202 assume a spherically symmetric object; the tutorial says multi-objective convex-relaxation versions exist for arbitrary shapes [355, 356]. | *Numbers:* Maximum absorption occurs at conjugate matching (sigma_abs = sigma_scat). The single-channel bounds are (sigma_scat,ext)_n=1 <= 3 lambda^2/(2pi) and (sigma_abs)_n=1 <= 3 lambda^2/(8pi) (Eq. 155). | *Applicability:* Exact per-channel floor on scattering at fixed absorption. Useful for lossy-cloak metrics and as a known result to reproduce. ERRATA I found: (a) as printed, Eq. 203 does not balance dimensionally (m^2 on the left, m on the right). From sigma_ext = (4pi/k) Im f(0) and |f(0)|^2 = sigma_scat D_s/(4pi), I get sigma_abs + sigma_scat <= lambda sqrt(sigma_scat D_s/pi), so the printed form is missing a factor of lambda (or assumes lambda-normalised units). (b) Bibliography entry [335], cited for Eq. 203, is 'Field behavior near a dielectric wedge' (Andersen & Solodukhov 1978), which looks like a wrong entry. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24738
- **Global all-spectrum cloaking bound: integrated extinction sum rule plus monotonicity (Monticone & Alu PRX 2013 [358]; Eqs. 167 and 188, Fig. 41)**: Integrated-extinction sum rule (Eq. 167): int_0^inf sigma_ext(lambda) d lambda = pi^2 (e^dagger alpha e), where alpha is the static polarizability tensor; magnetic media add h^dagger alpha_m h (fn. 46). Static electric and magnetic polarizabilities increase monotonically with static epsilon and mu, for isotropic [305] and anisotropic [306] media. Adding cloak material with eps(0) > eps0 and mu(0) > mu0 at every point therefore cannot lower the integrated extinction. The tutorial states this as a global bound: no passive, nondiamagnetic cloak can reduce the integrated scattering of the uncloaked object over the entire spectrum. Static ceiling for any object inside radius a: alpha_e(0) <= 4 pi eps0 a^3 (Eq. 188). | *Assumptions:* Linear, passive, lossless for simplicity (then sigma_ext = sigma_scat), and nondiamagnetic. Causality guarantees Re eps(0) >= eps0 (fn. 53), except for perfect conductors, where Re eps(0) -> -inf. mu(0) >= mu0 is NOT guaranteed, because static diamagnets exist. | *Numbers:* Eq. 167 constant: pi^2 e^dagger alpha e. Eq. 169 constant: pi omega_p^2 V/2. | *Applicability:* Rigorous, but only an all-frequency integral. The tutorial says explicitly that it does not constrain the continuous bandwidth over which scattering can stay below a nonzero level, nor how that bandwidth scales with size. The companion high-frequency rule int sigma_ext d omega = pi omega_p^2 V/2 (Eq. 169) ties the integral to total electron count. Corollary (my inference, not stated for cloaks in the tutorial): adding electrons through a cloak raises the frequency-integrated extinction. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24738
- **Spherical-wave KK relation and sum rule (Bernland, Gustafsson & Nordebo 2011 [360]; Eqs. 204-210): rigorous, but explicitly NOT a cloaking bound**: By causality, the outgoing partial wave can appear 2a/c earlier than in free space. The function with KK properties is therefore s_n(omega) e^{2i omega a/c}, not s_n itself: Re[s_n e^{2i omega a/c}] = 1 + (omega/pi) P int_{-inf}^{inf} Im[s_n(omega') e^{2i omega' a/c}]/(omega'(omega' - omega)) d omega' (Eq. 204). Upper-half-plane zeros are removed with a Blaschke product B_n = prod_i (1 - omega/omega_{n,i})/(1 - omega/omega_{n,i}^*) (Eq. 205), and h_n = -i log(s_n e^{2i omega a/c}/B_n) is Herglotz (Eq. 206). Sum rule: |int_0^inf omega^-2 ln|s_n| d omega| <= pi a/c (Eq. 208), equivalently |int_0^inf ln|s_n(lambda)| d lambda| <= 2 pi^2 a. If |s_n| <= S0 on [omega1, omega2] and 1 elsewhere, then |ln S0| B/pi <= k0 a (Eq. 210). The authors' verdict (p. 125): in the lossless case |s_n| = 1 on the real axis, so this bounds spherical-wave ABSORPTION, not invisibility; absorbing light generally makes an object easier to detect. | *Assumptions:* Passive, causal, spherically symmetric for the diagonal s_n. Off-diagonal s_{n,m} have Herglotz functions too, but their sum rules hit 'additional technical difficulties' (fn. 67). Tighter versions that use the static polarizability exist [360] (fn. 68). | *Numbers:* Example: S0 = 0.1 gives B <= pi k0 a/2.303 = 1.364 k0 a (my arithmetic from Eq. 210). | *Applicability:* Good for reproducing a known result (Rules item 1) and as a template. Not valid as a cloaking bound. The main obstruction (p. 129): with s_n = 2c_n + 1, a sum rule on ln|2c_n + 1| does not generally give a useful constraint on |c_n|. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24738

- **Local conservation-law constraints, SDP lift-and-relax, sparse QCQP and multi-scenario cross-constraints (Sec. 5.3, Eqs. 176-183, Figs. 31-33)**: Generalised constraints: phi^dagger D_i (Gamma0 + xi) phi = -phi^dagger D_i psi_inc for spatially local D_i (Eqs. 176-177). **Open problems**

- OQ16 (p. 131): general causality- and passivity-based size-bandwidth-performance limits for ANY cloak type, and rigorous limits for any imperfect invisibility effect given a fixed amount of matter in a prescribed volume (fn. 62 suggests electron count as the resource).
- OQ15 (p. 123): use the radius-dependent, phase-mixed KK relation for spherical coefficients, Re[s_n e^{2i omega a/c}] = 1 + (omega/pi) P int Im[s_n e^{2i omega' a/c}]/(omega'(omega' - omega)) d omega' (Eq. 204), to bound directional or multipolar scattering versus bandwidth. Related: OQ10 (encode causality and sum rules in conventional S matrices).
- Core technical obstruction named by the authors: Mie coefficients c_n = (s_n - 1)/2 obey only Eq. 204-type relations, and a sum rule on ln|2c_n + 1| does not constrain |c_n|. A rigorous lossless 3D cloaking bound needs a different Herglotz construction or a non-sum-rule route.
- Make the Monticone-Alu 3D estimate rigorous or refute it. My suggestion, not from the tutorial: for spherically or cylindrically symmetric layered cloaks the channels decouple into radial transmission lines, so a per-channel Bode-Fano/Darlington-type argument might be made exact. The catch is that the target is the free-space regular solution rather than a matched load, so classical Bode-Fano does not apply directly. This is directly testable with cloak2d.py and optimize_layers.
- Link the heuristic quality metrics (Craeye-Bhattacharya Qc, Chen Delta r0) to sigma_scat; the tutorial notes this link 'was not investigated'.
- Complex-frequency band averaging (Eq. 25) works only for metrics linear in the response (plane-wave extinction). Open: continuous-band bounds for quadratic, phase-insensitive (intensity) or angle-limited metrics, where analyticity is lost. Multi-scenario QCQP cross-constraints (Eq. 183) bound only the sampled frequencies or angles.
- For lossy cloaks, a lower bound on band-averaged extinction does not lower-bound band-averaged scattering, so metric choice (extinction versus scattering versus absorption-inclusive visibility) must be fixed per bound.
- Extend Strekha et al. 2024-style computable bounds beyond: one 2D circular object, an isotropic cloak of fixed chi, an extinction metric, and a tightness gap of about 10x. Also extend to anisotropic or magnetic materials, 3D, and the 'rearranged matter' invisibility case. Close the gap (tightness) and reduce SDP cost (N^3.5-N^4; OQ11 constraint selection; OQ12 FEM nonlocality).
- The global integrated bound (Eq. 167 plus monotonicity) excludes diamagnetic media (mu(0) < mu0) and perfect conductors (Re eps(0) -> -inf). Whether bandwidth-specific bounds survive these exceptions is open.
- Configurations outside every bound in the section: non-isolated objects (ground plane or carpet, high-index, lossy or diffusive backgrounds; fn. 59), and non-LTI systems (time-varying, nonlinear, active with prior knowledge of the incident field; fn. 65).
- Errata to account for when reproducing: Eq. 203 as printed lacks a factor of lambda (correct form by my derivation: sigma_abs + sigma_scat <= lambda sqrt(sigma_scat D_s/pi)); bibliography [335] is a wrong entry; p. 127 says 'parallel LC' where it means RC. Also, base_bounds_digest.md line 148 mis-renders the Monticone-Alu slab bound; the tutorial form is B <= lambda0/[d (eps_r - 1)|ln Gamma0|].

- **Strekha, Amaolo, Mohajan, Chao, Molesky & Rodriguez (PRA 110, 063513, 2024): Lagrange-dual bound on Lorentzian-averaged extinction of a fixed object plus an annular design region**: The bound is the minimum, over every polarization current |T_d> supported in the design region Omega_des, of P_ext(w~) = (1/2Z) Im[k~ <E_i| G_vac^-1 G_obj (V_obj|E_i> + P_des|T_d>)>]. It is subject to <E_d|P_m|T_d> - <T_d|U_m|T_d> = 0 for every subregion Omega_m in Omega_des, where U_m = P_des(chi_clk^-1dagger P_m - G_obj^dagger P_m)P_des and E_d = (G_vac T_obj + I)E_i is the field of the bare object. The real and imaginary parts of each constraint are local resistive and reactive power conservation. Evaluated at the complex frequency w~ = w0 + i*dw, the objective equals the Lorentzian-window average of P_ext(w), with window L = (dw/pi)/((w-w0)^2 + dw^2) (residue theorem). The concave dual gives a certified lower bound for every vacuum/chi_clk pattern in Omega_des. A multi-angle version (their Eq. 11) adds cross constraints <E_d^(a)|P_m|T_d^(b)> - <T_d^(a)|U_m|T_d^(b)> = 0 that tie the different incidence directions to one shared structure. | *Assumptions:* - Linear, passive, time-invariant, local, isotropic electric susceptibility.

- Vacuum background; plane-wave incidence from one or a few fixed directions.
- Computed only in 2D TM, with 2D FDFD and topology optimisation for comparison.
- The window is Lorentzian, not flat, and the objective is extinction (absorption plus scattering).
- The paper says the formalism carries over to 3D and anisotropic media, but does not demonstrate it. | *Numbers:* - Fig. 1: R_obj = lambda0/4, R_des = 3lambda0/4; chi_obj = 4 + 1e-3i or -4 + i; chi_clk = 3 or 5 (+1e-3i).

- **Jelinek, Gustafsson, Capek & Schab (Opt. Express 29, 26829, 2021; DOI 10.1364/OE.428536): monochromatic QCQP bounds for scattering-cancellation and field-zeroing cloaks**: The method uses method-of-moments currents I = alpha + beta*x. **Open problems**

- Flat-band lower bounds: convert Lorentzian (complex-frequency) extinction bounds into rigorous in-band bounds. One route is to subtract the maximum possible out-of-band contribution, using single-frequency upper bounds (k|chi|^2/Im chi per volume, or the channel bound (lambda^2/2pi)·sum(2n+1)). Another is multi-pole windows plus a positivity argument. Nobody has done either; without it, Strekha-type numbers do not certify flat-band invisibility.
- Scattering-only (phase-insensitive) lower bounds for a fixed object plus a design region. Candidate: sigma_sca >= pi sigma_ext^2/(lambda^2 D_s) with D_s <= N^2 + 2N (3D), or sigma_sca >= k sigma_ext^2/(4(2N+1)) (2D, derived here). This needs a rigorous tail estimate for orders n > N for an object of radius R. Neither is tested against cloak2d or optimize_layers yet; Jelinek et al. show the naive scattered-power QCQP degenerates.
- Angle-limited metrics: off-forward amplitudes are not tied to extinction, and eps = mu spheres have zero backscatter at all frequencies (Kerker 1983). There is no published lower bound on scattering into a cone for a fixed object plus design region. QCQP can handle it at single or sampled frequencies, but the material class must exclude dual-symmetric (magnetic) designs or the bound is trivially zero.
- A full-wave Herglotz analogue of Cassier-Milton, combining:
- rho(k) = S/k^2;
- rho(0) = gamma/4pi >= gamma_obj/4pi (monotonicity for non-diamagnetic cloaks);
- extinction-paradox high-frequency behaviour.
The sum rule alone permits zero in-band extinction, so an extra link is needed (e.g. the scattering-extinction or directivity relation, or channel truncation) before a passive-approximation convex programme can give in-band lower bounds.
- Material class at complex frequency: Strekha et al. specify chi(w~) directly. Mapping this to physical classes is needed to state the class (brief rule 2) and to falsify in the same class. Physical classes include non-dispersive real chi, bounded-strength Lorentz oscillators and bounded |chi| on the real axis. cloak2d uses frequency-independent indices, which are causal only when real.
- Scaling to electrically large objects: every computed bound covers objects of about lambda/4 to lambda (2D) or k a_u <= pi (3D). Dense SDP costs N^3.5 to N^4. OQ16 needs closed-form or extrapolable scaling in kR; Hashemi's <sigma>/s_g proportional to d holds only for transformation cloaks with bounded index. Sparse QCQP (Gertler et al.) or symmetry reduction (polar FDFD in dolphindes, angular-momentum blocks for annular regions) are the obvious levers.
- Tightness and constraint choice (tutorial OQ11): the Strekha bounds are within about 10x of designs; Jelinek's multilayer realisations depart from the bound above k a_u of about 1. Unknown: which local or redundant constraints close the gap for minimisation problems with a fixed object.

**Open problems**

- Is the Monticone–Alù 3D circuit bound (Eq. 2/4) actually valid? It has never been adversarially tested. Penetrable objects, harmonic coupling (non-spherical cloaks) and cloak material that changes the per-harmonic 'load' are outside its derivation. No 2D cylindrical version is published, so one must be derived before cloak2d/ceviche falsification.
- A rigorous analytic bound with explicit a/λ dependence beyond quasistatics does not exist. Hashemi 2012 gives only a constant-free 1/d scaling for transformation-optics cloaks. Candidate route: complex-frequency averaging (Im f(ω0+iΔω)) plus passivity/Herglotz constraints on the cloak region (Cassier–Milton style) plus a finite-size Green's-function bound.
- A bound over the full class of passive causal materials, rather than at a prescribed χ(ω0+iΔω) as in Strekha 2024, is missing. Specify the material class instead, e.g. static ε, an f-sum/plasma-frequency budget ∫σ_ext dω = πω_p²V/2, or a Herglotz class with bounded |χ|, so that the bound is in terms of 'amount of matter' (OQ16).
- Phase-insensitive, angle-limited, backscatter-only and forward-only metrics have no bounds. Hashemi 2012 lists them explicitly as open; OQ15 asks whether the spherical-coefficient causality relation (Eq. 204) yields multipolar or directional bandwidth bounds. Directional topology-optimized designs reach <1-5% residual scattering, far below omnidirectional performance.
- The tightness gap at ka ≳ 1 is unknown. Jelinek et al. cannot tell whether their bound is loose or their designs weak. Strekha's gap is about 10x only at λ/4 objects. Bound-vs-design gaps for Monticone–Alù are given only graphically (TO cloak 'close' for >10 dB at a = λ; 2-metasurface cloak 6 dB/20%).
- The lossless large-bandwidth limit for anisotropic or unbounded-contrast materials is open. Strekha's positive floor as loss → 0 comes from bounded isotropic χ (consistent with Hashemi 2011's index bound b). How bandwidth alone (lossless, anisotropic) limits extinction at finite size is not quantified.
- Rigorous treatment of delay–loss for ground-plane cloaks, which escape isolated-object causality limits, is missing; Hashemi 2010 is a 1D scaling argument with '≪'. Bounds that combine loss, bandwidth and size into one inequality are open.
- Quasistatic bounds compare against α(∞), the bare-object polarizability, which assumes a nondispersive object (CMW 2026 Cor. 65 partly relaxes this). 2D (cylinder, per-unit-length) analogues of Cassier–Milton and CMW are not published but look straightforward. They would be a cheap reproduce-first target for the OptiLex 2D verifier.
